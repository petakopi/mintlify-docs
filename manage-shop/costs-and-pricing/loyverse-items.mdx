<Info>Visible only when your shop is connected to **Loyverse POS**. Not connected yet? See [Loyverse integration](/manage-shop/loyverse) for the connect flow.</Info>

Without linking: [Pulse](/manage-shop/pulse/overview) shows total revenue. With linking: the [Recipes index](/manage-shop/costs-and-pricing/recipes) shows per-recipe sales count, revenue, and gross margin.

## Map what sells first

The page opens on **Best sellers** — variants ranked by units sold in the last 30 days, with a **Last sold** column beside it. A catalog of 90 variants usually has 30-odd carrying the business and a long tail that hasn't sold in months; this is how you tell them apart before spending an evening mapping.

| Column | What it tells you |
|---|---|
| **Sold** | Units rung up in the selected period, across every variation. The higher the number, the sooner mapping it pays off. |
| **Price** | What it last rang up for, per unit, before any discount. |
| **Last sold** | When it last appeared on a receipt. Anything quiet for 90 days reads **not in 90d** — usually a discontinued item you can skip entirely. |

Switch to **A–Z** when you're hunting for one specific variant rather than working down the list.

### Choosing the period

**This month / Last 30 days / Last 90 days** sets what the Sold column counts. **This month** is the calendar month, so it resets on the 1st — useful when you're working through the catalog in sittings and only want to see what's rung up since you last looked.

**Last sold** ignores the period. It always looks back 90 days, because it answers a different question: not "how much", but "is this still on the menu at all".

### The counters

The four tiles count **what sold in the selected period**, and each one filters the table to match:

| Tile | What it counts |
|---|---|
| **Sold** | Items with at least one sale in the period |
| **Unmapped** | Of those, the ones still without a recipe — your actual to-do list |
| **Mapped** | Of those, the ones already done |
| **Not sold** | Everything else in the catalog |

Unmapped deliberately excludes items that didn't sell. A shop with 91 catalog items and 12 active ones has 12 decisions to make this month, not 91 — a backlog you can never clear is one you stop reading.

<Note>Both columns count **receipts, not ringgit** — you're ranking by how often you'd use the recipe, and revenue would push a RM 45 bag of beans above a latte you sell 200 of. Refunds are ignored: a refund means a transaction was reversed, not that the drink is unpopular.</Note>

The figures appear whether or not a variant is mapped — they're read from your receipt history directly, so they're there from the moment Loyverse is connected and backfilled.

## Variations

If you price through **modifiers** — one `Cafe Latte` item with `hot` and `ice` options rather than two separate items — each row shows a **N variations** link. Open it to see every combination customers have actually ordered, with its own sold count and price.

This is where the price column earns its place:

| Cafe Latte | Sold (30d) | Price |
|---|---|---|
| hot | 21 | RM 10.90 |
| ice | 18 | RM 11.90 |

A gap like that says the two aren't the same drink — iced needs ice, often more milk, sometimes a different cup — so costing them as one recipe would quietly overstate the margin on one and understate the other.

### Variations follow the base row

You don't map every combination. A variation shows **Follows base**, meaning it counts against whatever recipe you mapped the item to. Map `Cafe Latte` once and all 20 of its variations are covered.

Override a variation only where the ingredients genuinely differ. Open its picker and pick a recipe, and only that combination changes — everything else keeps following the base. The item row then shows how many you've costed separately.

<Tip>Start by overriding hot vs iced on your top few drinks. That's usually where the real cost difference sits; sizes and syrups matter less than they look.</Tip>

<Warning>Price differences don't always mean recipe differences. Delivery-platform orders often carry their own higher-priced modifier options to cover commission — same drink, different price. Compare the option names, not just the figures.</Warning>

## How to map (one-time per variant)

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open Loyverse Items">
    Sidebar → **Loyverse Items**. Each row is a Loyverse variant (e.g. "Latte (Hot)", "Latte (Iced)") grouped by parent item.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Pick a Recipe">
    From the dropdown next to each variant. **Multiple variants can map to the same Recipe** — Hot + Iced Latte → one "Latte" recipe.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Sync now (if needed)">
    The **Sync now** button at the top pulls the latest catalog from Loyverse. 5-minute cooldown between syncs to stay polite to the API.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## What you get after mapping

- [Recipes index](/manage-shop/costs-and-pricing/recipes) fills in three columns: **Sold this month**, **Revenue**, **Gross margin**. They only render in the index's **Full** view — see [Minimal and Full views](/manage-shop/costs-and-pricing/recipes#minimal-and-full-views) if the index looks unchanged after mapping.
- Backfill runs automatically — historical receipts that match the variant get attributed to the recipe within seconds.
- The Recipes form shows a read-only **"Linked Loyverse items"** block listing what's currently pointing at each recipe.

## Variants without a recipe

- Still count toward your total [Pulse](/manage-shop/pulse/overview) revenue.
- You just don't get the per-recipe breakdown for them.
- Sidebar shows an **"(N unmapped)"** badge so you don't forget.

Mapping is incremental: cover your top sellers first — the [Best sellers sort](#map-what-sells-first) puts them at the top — and come back to rare items later. The sidebar badge is your nudge; it ticks down as you map, but having unmapped variants doesn't break anything.
