The Calculator is the "what should I be charging this month?" page. Each month is its own plan: use **‹ Previous month** and **Next month ›** beside the page title to move between months.

The answer sits at the top. Your assumptions sit below it, and the answer updates as you type.

## The answer

<Steps>
  <Step title="Can your prices cover fixed costs?">
    A verdict pill (**Viable**, **Stretch**, **Not viable** or **Set a price**), the line "Breakeven *n* cups a month vs *planned* planned", and one sentence on what to do next. See [Can your prices cover fixed costs?](#can-your-prices-cover-fixed-costs) below.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Suggested prices">
    One row per recipe: **Suggested** (what you'd need to charge), **Menu** (what you charge today) and **Change** (the gap between them).
  </Step>
  <Step title="How it's worked out">
    Closed by default. Open it to see the fixed cost per cup, breakeven sales, average profit per cup and total fixed costs. See [How it's worked out](#how-its-worked-out).
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Your assumptions

One card, three parts. Press **Save settings** at the bottom when you're done.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Cups a month">
    Type the number of cups (or items) you expect to sell. Not sure? Press **Estimate from seats instead** and fill in seats, hours per day, time each customer stays, how full the shop is, items per customer and days open. Press **Enter cups directly instead** to go back. A month saved with the seats estimate opens with it showing.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Fixed costs">
    One row per monthly cost: name, category (Labour / Overhead / Other), amount. See the [example breakdown below](#example-fixed-costs).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Target margin %">
    The slice of every sale you want to keep as profit.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Example fixed costs

For the running Hot Coffee Latte example, here's a realistic breakdown for a small Malaysian coffee shop:

| Name | Category | Amount |
|---|---|---|
| Rent | Overhead | RM 2,000 |
| Wages — Barista | Labour | RM 1,800 |
| Utilities (electric + water) | Overhead | RM 600 |
| Internet | Overhead | RM 100 |
| POS subscription | Overhead | RM 100 |
| Cleaning supplies | Other | RM 200 |
| Maintenance & misc | Other | RM 100 |
| | **Total** | **RM 4,900** |

The categories don't change the math — the Calculator adds everything into one fixed-cost number. They help you organise this plan. They do not create Performance expense records or change your [legacy expenses](/manage-shop/performance/legacy-expenses).

This RM 4,900 total is what feeds the **fixed cost per cup** in the Hot Coffee Latte example below: RM 4,900 ÷ 2,500 cups = RM 1.96 per cup.

## Suggested prices

The **Suggested prices** table puts three columns side by side — **Suggested** (what you'd need to charge to hit your target margin), **Menu** (what you charge today, from the recipe's [Menu price](/manage-shop/costs-and-pricing/recipes#previously-saved-pricing-values)), and **Change**, the gap between them as a percentage. Change is red when your menu price is more than 5% below suggested, amber within 5%, green when it meets or beats it. Scanning that column is the fastest way to spot which drinks are underpriced.

A recipe with no ingredient cost yet shows **Add ingredient cost** instead of a price.

### Example: Hot Coffee Latte

Continuing the running example from [Recipes](/manage-shop/costs-and-pricing/recipes):

| Input | Value |
|---|---|
| Ingredient cost per cup (from the recipe's items) | RM 3.10 |
| Fixed cost per cup (RM 4,900 fixed costs ÷ 2,500 cups) | RM 1.96 |
| Target margin | 30% |

Suggested price: `(3.10 + 1.96) ÷ (1 − 0.30) = RM 7.23`

Round to a clean menu number (RM 7, RM 7.50, RM 8). The Calculator price (RM 7.23 here) is lower than the Quick price (RM 12.40 from the [Recipes example](/manage-shop/costs-and-pricing/recipes#quick-price-for-this-recipe)) because the two answer different questions — Quick price targets an ingredient-cost ratio, Calculator targets a margin after fixed costs.

### Why divide by (1 − margin)?

If you want to keep 30% of every sale as profit, the costs have to fit in the other 70%:

`price × 0.70 = ingredient cost + fixed cost per cup`

Rearranging:

`price = (ingredient cost + fixed cost per cup) ÷ (1 − margin)`

If you change target margin from 30% to 20%, the divisor goes from 0.70 to 0.80 and the suggested price drops accordingly.

## Can your prices cover fixed costs?

Suggested prices answer "what *should* I charge?" — but the menu price you charge today might be different. This card tells you whether your real menu prices pay your fixed costs at the cups you plan to sell.

It uses the previously saved **Menu price (RM)** on each [Recipe](/manage-shop/costs-and-pricing/recipes#previously-saved-pricing-values). For every recipe with a menu price, it works out the profit per cup (`menu price − ingredient cost`), averages it, and divides your fixed costs by that average. The result is **breakeven** — the cups a month you need to sell to pay every fixed cost at today's prices. It sits next to the cups you planned.

| Verdict | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Viable** | Breakeven is at or below the cups you plan to sell. |
| **Stretch** | Breakeven is above your plan. Raise prices, sell more cups, or cut fixed costs. |
| **Not viable** | On average, your menu prices don't cover the ingredient cost per cup. Every cup loses money before fixed costs. |
| **Set a price** | No recipe has a previously saved menu price, so the check can't run. Use Performance for the current monthly result. |

The verdict updates as you edit fixed costs or cups a month. It uses legacy recipe prices; changing a storefront product price does not update them.

<Note>Recipe menu prices are legacy saved values and are no longer editable on the recipe form. Storefront product prices are separate. See [previously saved pricing values](/manage-shop/costs-and-pricing/recipes#previously-saved-pricing-values).</Note>

## How it's worked out

Open **How it's worked out** under the answer to see four numbers, one per line:

| Line | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Fixed cost per cup** | `total fixed costs ÷ cups a month`. The slice of rent and wages each cup has to carry. |
| **Breakeven sales** | Sales (RM) a month at which profit is zero. See [Breakeven sales](#breakeven-sales). |
| **Average profit per cup** | Menu price minus ingredient cost, averaged across recipes with a menu price. |
| **Total fixed costs** | Every fixed-cost row, added up. |

If **fixed cost per cup** looks wrong, either the cups a month or one of your fixed-cost rows is the culprit.

### Breakeven sales

```
breakeven_sales = fixed_costs × per_cup_price ÷ profit_per_cup
where profit_per_cup = per_cup_price − avg_ingredient_cost
```

1. **Profit per cup** = `per_cup_price − avg_ingredient_cost`. What's left of each cup's price after the ingredients — available to pay fixed costs.
2. **Cups needed to pay fixed costs** = `fixed_costs ÷ profit_per_cup`. Multiply by `per_cup_price` to get breakeven sales.

The **price** prefers your recipes' menu prices — averaged across recipes that have one — and falls back to the Calculator's suggested price when no recipe has a menu price. That's why a menu price *below* suggested *raises* breakeven: less profit per cup means you need more cups to pay the same fixed costs. These are planning numbers; Performance calculates its monthly result from separate sales and cost evidence.

### Where each input comes from

| Variable | Where you set it | Page |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `fixed_costs` | **Fixed costs** — every row added up | Calculator |
| `cups a month` | **Cups a month** — typed in, or estimated from seats | Calculator |
| `target_margin` | **Target margin** — only used in the suggested-price fallback | Calculator |
| `per_cup_price` *(menu-price path)* | Previously saved recipe menu prices, averaged | Legacy recipe data |
| `recipe_line.quantity` | Each recipe's ingredient line ("18g of beans") | Recipes → edit |
| `item.unit_cost` | Per-item cost ("RM 0.20 per gram of beans") | Items → edit |

`avg_ingredient_cost` is worked out, not entered: each recipe's ingredient cost = sum of `quantity × item.unit_cost` across its lines, then averaged across all recipes.

## Reading the result

- **Suggested price feels too high vs market.** Fixed cost per cup is the lever — rent or wages are too high for this many cups. Sell more cups or cut fixed costs.
- **Verdict says Stretch.** At today's prices you need more cups than you planned. Revisit your cup estimate, prices or fixed costs before you ship the menu.
- **Suggested price is well below market.** Healthy headroom. Keep prices low to win volume, or hold near market and bank the extra profit.

### Benchmarks for a Malaysian coffee shop

Rough numbers, gathered from talking to owners — your context will vary:

| Metric | Healthy range |
|---|---|
| Ingredient cost (% of sales) | 25–35% |
| Left after ingredients (% of sales) | 65–75% |
| Profit after rent + wages (% of sales) | 10–20% |
| Fixed cost per cup | RM 1.50 – RM 3.00 |

If your numbers are way outside these, dig in — there's usually a story (premium beans pushing ingredient cost up, few cups making fixed cost per cup balloon, etc.).

## Per-month workflow

| Action | What happens |
|---|---|
| Visit a new month for the first time | Form pre-fills from your most recent prior month, with a "Cloned from April 2026" cue at the top. |
| Edit and save | Updates only that month. |
| Visit an old month | Use **‹ Previous month**. Shows what you had set then (or blank if you never set it up). |
| Edit a past month | One-time confirmation: saving changes that month's suggested prices and cost history. |
| Plan ahead (future month) | Use **Next month ›**. A soft warning is shown. |
| **Apply forward to the next N months** | Open the **⋯** menu beside the title and choose **Apply forward…**. Copies the saved month's settings forward, up to 11 months. Not offered on future months. |
