---
title: "5. Review your monthly answer"
description: "Understand surplus, shortfall, and incomplete results."
---

**Outcome:** you know whether recorded sales cover the month’s included costs, and how reliable that answer is.

Before starting, complete [costing](/manage-shop/performance/costing), [expenses](/manage-shop/performance/expenses), and [sales evidence](/manage-shop/performance/sales). Open **Performance → Overview**. Open **Monthly review** for cost details and confirmations.

## Reporting time

Open **Performance → Settings**. Choose **Daily report time** as a whole hour in Malaysia time. This closes your reporting day. It is independent of your opening hours, including shops with multiple opening periods.

For a 3am cutoff, sales before 3am belong to the preceding reporting day. An event exactly at 3am belongs to the new day. September ends at 3am on 1 October.

Preview the boundaries before confirming. The initial setting also applies to historical periods. Later changes begin next reporting month and do not move old sales between periods.

## Review inputs

Open **Performance → Monthly review**.

1. Check that all shop sales for the period are included.
2. Review each operating-cost category, confirming zero only where correct.
3. Check outstanding draft invoices and labour costs.
4. Classify costs as fixed or variable, splitting mixed costs.
5. Save the monthly review after resolving missing information.

Fixed costs cover the full month; variable costs cover sales so far. Wages include employer costs and any recorded owner salary. Unpaid owner labour and owner withdrawals are excluded.

Review a month after it ends: the final sign-off is the review of a closed month. You can review an open month for a so-far answer, but the next sale or cost makes it stale, so you confirm once more after the month closes. Overview reminds you when the previous month has ended and still needs review.

If another sales channel is missing, leave sales coverage unconfirmed. Input changes or the month closing can require a fresh review.

## Read the answer

| Result | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Surplus | Included net sales exceed included consumption and operating costs |
| Shortfall | Included costs exceed net sales; the gap remains uncovered |
| Incomplete | Missing information prevents a reliable whole-shop answer |

The answer is an **estimated operating result**, not cash in the bank or final net profit. It excludes depreciation, loan interest, income tax, and unrecorded waste or stock differences.

## Understand the included costs

In **Monthly review**, open **Where the included costs went** to see operating categories ordered by known amount. Each category shows actual, estimated, confirmed-zero, and unknown entries. Expand its sources to inspect the bills or estimates.

Unknown-only categories appear after known amounts and display **Unknown**. The largest known amount is not proof of overspending. Ingredient consumption is shown separately, including confirmed preparation losses; ingredient purchases are not deducted again.

## Understand the monthly numbers

Overview shows order count and average order value. **Average order value** divides gross sales, including recorded rounding, by distinct orders sold in the selected reporting month. POS sales count when paid; online orders when completed. It includes zero-value orders and rounds to the nearest sen. Refunds and allowances reduce net monthly sales separately; a later refund does not rewrite the original order's value.

An open month shows the average **as of** the displayed time. With no orders, or unresolved sales evidence, the average is unavailable. Missing recipe costs or wages do not hide an otherwise reliable sales average. A payment warning stays visible because completing an order is not proof of cash received.

Some older order histories have only a general warning. Their sales evidence cannot be separated reliably from costing gaps, so Performance asks for evidence review before showing an average.

In **Monthly review**, two cost percentages appear for a **completed, reviewed and complete month with positive net sales**:

- **Estimated ingredient and packaging consumption %** = recorded recipe-estimated consumption ÷ net sales × 100. Confirmed preparation losses are included and shown separately within consumption. Purchases are excluded.
- **Wages %** = included wage-category costs ÷ net sales × 100. Confirm employer costs and any recorded owner salary in the review. Unpaid owner labour and withdrawals are excluded.

Open months, incomplete or stale reviews, and non-positive net sales have no cost percentages. Displayed shares round to one decimal place and can exceed 100%; they are arithmetic shares, not targets. Use the links beside the figures to inspect orders, consumption and wage entries.

For example, five orders sell RM100 and a separate RM20 refund gives RM80 net sales and RM20 average order value. RM30 estimated consumption, including RM5 confirmed preparation losses, is 37.5% of net sales. RM20 recorded wages is 25%. A refund-only month has no order average.

## Café example

Assume all 100 lattes were sold and paid, every latte cost RM 3, and no sales, refunds, losses, or operating costs are missing.

| Input | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| Net sales: 100 × RM 10 | RM 1,000 |
| Ingredients and packaging: 100 × RM 3 | RM 300 |
| Rent + utilities estimate + fees | RM 650 |
| Estimated surplus | **RM 50** |

When the RM 180 utilities bill replaces the RM 100 estimate, operating costs become RM 730. The answer becomes **RM 30 shortfall**: RM 1,000 − RM 300 − RM 730.

## Compare a completed month

When both this month and the previous month have ended and each has a current review of complete inputs, Overview shows the change in net sales, estimated consumption, and operating costs.

The largest change is the biggest arithmetic difference. It does not state a cause. A previous amount of zero or a negative previous amount has no percentage. Months that use different reporting times are not compared. Saved email reports keep the figures they were sent with.

## Additional sales

For an open month, Performance may estimate the extra sales needed to cover a gap. It uses the month’s complete sales mix and explicitly classified variable costs.

Confirm that this mix represents likely additional sales before relying on the estimate. If inputs are incomplete, refunds affect the period, or the contribution margin cannot support a useful estimate, the target is unavailable. A complete result can still show the gap.

**Ready when:** the review is current and you understand any estimates or remaining gaps.
