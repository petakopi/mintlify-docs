---
title: "Check your menu prices"
description: "See which products are underpriced, what to charge instead, and and how each one sells."
---

**Outcome:** you know which prices to change, to what, and how each product sells.

Open **Costs & Pricing → Menu Pricing**. Every active product is listed under its category with one verdict and one action. Tap a product to see its cost, price, suggested price, units sold, and sales mix. Only one product is open at a time. The chips under the title (**All**, **To raise**, **To check**, **Needs cost**, **Fine**) show how many products need each action. Tap one to list only those. Type in the search box above them to find a product by name; it works together with the chips. Prices here are **before SST**.

## Read the verdicts and actions

| Verdict | Meaning | What the action says |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Underpriced | The price is more than 5% below the target price | Raise to RM 10.00 (+RM 1.00) |
| On target | The price is within 5% of the target price | Keep at RM 9.50 |
| Above target | The price is more than 5% above the target and a lower price would still hit the target | Keep at RM 11.00 if it sells well, otherwise Could drop to RM 10.00 (−RM 1.00) |
| Needs cost | The product has no cost yet | Add a cost |
| Needs fixed costs | Cost + overhead + margin is selected but no month has been reviewed | Review a month first |

The action line is a link to the product, where you change the price. The action is always about price, one instruction per product. A product above target that sells well shows **Keep at RM 11.00**, because that is pricing power, not a problem.

The suggested price is the target price rounded up to the next 50 sen. The page never changes a price for you. The info button next to Suggested price in an opened product shows the working, step by step, with your own numbers.

## Where the cost comes from

Cost is the sales-weighted average of what each sale actually cost, over the period you picked. If the product has not sold in that period, the page uses its Product Costing setup cost instead. A product with options can cost different amounts per sale, so a setup cost shows as **Cost from**.

## Choose how the target is set

Tap **Settings** beside the period picker to change the method. Settings save for the whole shop. Cancel closes the window without saving.

**Cost as % of price** (the default)

```text
target price = cost ÷ target %
```

Worked example: a latte costs RM 3.30 and the target is 30%. RM 3.30 ÷ 0.30 = **RM 11.00**. If it sells at RM 9.50 the verdict is Underpriced, and the suggested price is RM 11.00.

Drinks start at 30% and food at 35%. Food is guessed from the category name. Set your own percentage per category.

**Cost + overhead + margin**

```text
target price = (cost + fixed cost per item) ÷ (1 − margin)
```

Fixed cost per item is your monthly fixed costs, taken from the latest month you reviewed in Monthly review, divided by items sold per month. Leave **Items sold per month** blank to use what you actually sold in the period, scaled to 30 days. Type a number to plan for a different volume. Worked example: cost RM 3.30, fixed cost RM 2.00 per item, 20% margin gives (3.30 + 2.00) ÷ 0.80 = RM 6.63, suggested RM 7.00.

## Change the period

Use the menu beside the title: **Last 30 days**, **Last 90 days**, and, once you have chosen a Daily report time, **This month** and **Last month**. The line under the title shows how many items were sold in the period. Costs, units sold, popularity, and the realised price all follow it.

## Read the sales mix

Once a product has sales, the page compares it with the rest of its category. Tap the product to read it. **Sells well** means it sold at least 70% of an equal share of its category. **Earns well** means it earns at least the category's average per item. The open product shows a short reason such as "Sells well, earns little".

| Sales mix | Sells well | Earns well |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sells well, earns well | Yes | Yes |
| Sells well, earns little | Yes | No |
| Earns well, sells slowly | No | Yes |
| Slow seller, thin margin | No | No |

Products with no sales sink to the bottom of their category. Products without a cost get no sales mix.

## Good to know

- Margins use the price customers actually paid per item, averaged over the period. Voucher discounts are taken off the whole order, not the item, so they are not in that price.
- Figures are shown rounded to the sen. A product can sit at the edge of a 5% band, so the verdict may disagree with a hand calculation by a sen.
- Option price adjustments are not part of the target. Compare the target with the base price.
